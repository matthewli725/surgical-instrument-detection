

# General Description

- Generally, critical design reviews (CDR) are a formal checkpoint in the design process to ensure that designs being generated for the final product fit the requirements of all interested parties. They are typically attended by other engineers, managers, and customers who evaluate the suitability of the presented design. CDRs are NOT one-way presentations. They involve interaction between all parties present to provide feedback/criticisms and guarantee that the design is prepared to proceed to the next stage of the design cycle. They are intended to identify any mistakes, concerns, or areas for optimization or further improvement in the presented designs.

- In this class, we are using the CDR to formally demonstrate that your designs are developing at a sufficient pace to have a fully qualified product design that meets the expected requirements by the end of the quarter. Because we are constrained by class schedules and time limits, we will be expecting significant interaction via written forum (BruinLearn). We will also be using the CDR as an opportunity to refine your project scopes to guarantee success by the final design review (FDR) at the end of the quarter.

# Forms and Due Dates

## Oral Presentation

- **Content**– The oral presentation will particularly focus on differences from your PDR, and bring up issues on which you seek further inputs from the instructor / TAs / peers.  Your goal is not to assert that everything is going according to plan, but rather highlight where it is not, and discuss strategies to meet requirements and deadlines.  It should be designed to spawn a conversation that leads to a clearer understanding of stakeholder expectations and requirements during your FDR deliverables.

- **Form**– In-class presentation, 20-25 minutes (followed by 5 mins of questions): 1-2 minutes introduction/**brief** project definition recap; ~5 minutes comprehensive technical status updates; ~10 minutes description of concerns / risks / remaining plan, including refinements to scope; ~5 minutes detailed description of expected final design deliverables.

- **Involvement**– All team members are expected to speak/present on the work they have done. A team leader or any combination of team members can present the introduction/concerns/upcoming plans.

- **Due date**– Slides due \due on BruinLearn, **and also** posted to a new forum discussion at that same time.

- **Note 1**– This oral presentation is **instead of** the weekly deliverables update presentation you would normally present at that time (you do NOT need to submit those slides).

- **Note 2**– Take advantage of the media we are using.  As always, static text, images, or screenshots are of particularly low value.  Rather, employ live demos of physical artifacts, code execution, running CAD and/or simulations to demonstrate and communicate the functionality of what you have developed.

## Revised Milestone Deliverables / Expectations

- **Content**– A revised deliverables/task list as described in section 3.4 below.

- **Form**– Gitlab issues and milestones as described in Part 9 of the PDR assignment document.

- **Due date**– \due via the Gitlab issue tracker.

## BruinLearn Interaction

- **Content 1**– Each student is required to individually leave meaningful engineering feedback on the CDR presentations of at least 4 other teams.  Highlight unaddressed risks, identify gaps between requirements and capabilities, and/or otherwise challenge the presented narrative.

- **Content 2**– Each team must respond to all feedback left on their CDR oral presentations.  Note that responses are generally supported by additional engineering deliverables.

- **Content 3--n**– Continue the interaction until a resolution is reached.

- **Form**– Posts/comments on the BruinLearn forums

- **Due date**– The first round of feedback must be posted by \feedbackdue. Teams must have posted responses to that first set of feedback by \replydue.

## Written Report

- **Content**– The written report will include information on the work you have completed thus far on your project, and any changes required to your project scope. The length of the written report is not specified, but it should thoroughly document the work you have done thus far and its consequences on the project as a whole. It should include the outcomes of the iterative feedback with your colleagues.  The work documented in the CDR will be carried through to your FDR, and thus can be seen as the first sections of your final report.

- **Form**– Written report, Unlimited number of pages

- **Due date**– PDF due \paperdue on BruinLearn.

# Report Content

## Part 1: Project Definition (from PDR)

The first portion of the CDR is to define the project. This was largely accomplished in the PDR, and content can be directly copied/adapted from the PDR where appropriate (i.e. unchanged), and otherwise **updated to reflect your latest understanding**.  To reiterate, this section should define the problem, the scope of the solution being considered, the requirements/specifications of the solution, and the process by which a solution was selected to solve the problem given the requirements/specifications. Any background information needed to understand the problem and/or solution should be presented. Note: this section should be **consistent** with your final design plans---any changes to your scope must be reflected in your documentation/diagrams.

## Part 2: Design/Engineering Work (from this quarter thus far)

 The second portion of your CDR is to document all the engineering work you have accomplished since the PDR. This includes summaries of the deliverables relevant to demonstrating the capabilities of your design as have been specified in your PDR and deliverables lists. **In addition to what you have done, be sure to detail why you did it the way you did (why you made the design choices you did), and how the deliverable has adequately demonstrated the capability of your design.** You should particularly describe any design decisions that resulted in deviations from the solution presented in the PDR---what information justified the change made, how did you obtain said information, and how did you verify it. It should be clear in both the written and oral report which team member(s) were responsible the tasks that led to each deliverable.

## Part 3: Unknowns and Concerns (as of now)

This section of your CDR is linked to risk assessment, and essentially describes what is yet to be determined that may affect the ability of the final product to meet capabilities and requirements. Here you should detail any elements of the design that are still unknown, or of particular concern. This may include specifications of individual elements of the design, aspects of integration, or other questions that can not be answered given the current state of the design. In addition to describing what these unknowns/questions are, you should detail what is preventing them from being knowable/answerable now or what they depend on. This section is notably **not** for aspects of your design that you can be reasonably confident depend only on investing sufficient time/energy.

## Part 4: Refinement of Scope and Future Work (to be completed in the remainder of this quarter)

This section refines and describes the remaining work to be completed on your projects to answer the unknowns/concerns and finish the tasks necessary to demonstrate all required capabilities. There are generally two scenarios that are not mutually exclusive and generally occur on a gradient:

- **Scenario 1**: Your project (as a whole, or in specific aspects) is proceeding as expected. Your scope and capabilities are still feasible. In this case, expectations for your final outcome demonstrations do not need to be updated. Similarly, your Gantt chart has not undergone major revision, but has increased in detail. This section will therefore be a reiteration of your current plan (primarily as a Gantt chart, but using paragraphs and/or deliverables/task lists for clarity where appropriate) for the remainder of the quarter at a finer resolution.

- **Scenario 2**: Your project (as a whole, or in specific aspects) is not proceeding as expected. Your scope and/or capabilities require revision. In this case, modify your weekly milestone deliverables to reflect the revised scope and/or capabilities. Your Gantt chart should similarly be revised, and new expectations for your final outcomes must be clearly identified. Any changes to your scope must be discussed with and approved by the instructors/TAs before this CDR presentation. This section will therefore detail your revised plan (primarily as a Gantt chart, but using paragraphs and/or deliverables/task lists for clarity where appropriate) for the remainder of the quarter. Additionally, it must provide good reason(s) for why each scope/capability must be changed, and why the new scope/capability is reasonable and relevant.

In either case, you should clearly define your final design deliverables in terms of the decisions you will present and support during your FDR.  Set clear expectations for your final demonstrations and justify how they will convince sceptical stakeholders that your presented product design will accomplish its necessary functionality.
